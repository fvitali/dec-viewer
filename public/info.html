What is DEC Viewer?

DEC Viewer is a web application providing access to a reasoner for cognitive worlds over a quad store using Fuseki 4.8.0.

DEC cognitive worlds

DEC stands for Doxastic, Epistemic, and Conjectural, three types of cognitive worlds meant to characterize the subjective state of mind of one or more actors holding beliefs, concepts and hypotheses over reality. Examples are "Alice believes that John is a painter" or "Bruce knows that painters are artists." or "Catherine supposes John painted 'Still life #13'." The DEC Reasoner is able to carry out inferences on objective and subjective assertions like these when expressed in RDF.

Cognitive worlds are sets of statements, isolated from each other (and, in general, from reality), which are held for true, or abstractly considered for evaluation, by a cognitive agent or within a given cognitive contest. The exact nature of the cognitive world is given by a predicate connecting the agent (or the context) to the cognitive world, and is determined by the nature of the governing verb:

  • Doxastic worlds represent subjective stances that are independent of reality, and possibly in contrast with it. These stances include beliefs, opinions or delusions. Example governing verbs are "believe", "be convinced" or "think". There is no connection between what is held as true in a doxastic cognitive world and what is true in reality.
  • Epistemic worlds represent subjective stances that have access to a (possibly limited) fragment of reality. These stances include knowledge, discoveries, judgments. Example governing verbs are "know" "be aware" or even "decide". Everything in an epistemic cognitive world is true, but there may be some truths that are not available inside it.
  • Conjectural worlds represent subjective stances that are totally based on reality, and include some further assertions that are neither true nor false in reality. These stances include conjectures, hypothesis and suppositions. Example governing verbs are "suppose", "consider", "suspect", "postulate" or "speculate". Everything that is true is available in a conjectural cognitive world, but there may be some additional assertions available inside it that are neither true nor false in reality.

The nature of the cognitive world, therefore, determines the reciprocal availability of facts between reality and the world. We call this availability "permeation": statements belonging to a world are added, i.e., permeate, into another. Thus, doxastic worlds do not permeate into reality, nor reality permeates into doxastic worlds; epistemic worlds permeate into reality, but not viceversa; and reality permeates into conjectural worlds, but not viceversa.

Disagreements, delusions and opinions

Separation and permeation of statements within cognitive worlds allow to naturally represent disagreements, opinions, and delusions as reciprocal stances between cognitive worlds. Specifically, we say that:

  • Two cognitive worlds disagree if they cannot be or become true at the same time, i.e., if they contain reciprocally inconsistent statements. For instance, "Catherine supposes that John painted 'Still life #13'." and "David believes that Mary painted 'Still life #13'." disagree (if we assume that 'Still life #13' is the same painting in both statements, that John and Mary are different individuals and that they did not work together).
  • A cognitive world is delusional if it contains at least one statement that is inconsistent with reality. For instance, "Emily thinks that the Mona Lisa painting is at the Uffizi Museum" is a delusional statement, since it is a fact that Mona Lisa is at the Louvre Museum, and Louvre and Uffizi are different museums.
  • A cognitive world is an opinion if it contains statement that are, and will always be, neither true nor false in reality. For instance, "Frank thinks that pizza is better than sushi" is an opinion because neither this assertion nor its negation will ever be accepted as a fact.

Other cognitive worlds

Doxastic, Epistemic and Conjectural are the most meaningful cognitive worlds supported by the DEC Reasoner, but there are three further worlds that must be considered:

  • Shared worlds: every statement placed inside a shared world is also available in all other cognitive worlds (and reality), without the need to repeat the statement more than once. For instance, "Mona Lisa is a painting" should probably belong to a shared world. Shared world do not contain all the facts, but only all the statements that every world, including delusional ones, is aware of.
  • Reality: every statement placed outside of any cognitive world belong to a special cognitive world, that we call reality. Under the DEC reasoning model, statements of reality, or facts, are true or false regardless of how or whether they are considered as such in any other cognitive world.
  • Verbatim worlds represent subjective stances that must remain precisely as presented, with no reasoning allowed, nor permeations from other worlds. These stances include the exact representation of speeches and writings. Example governing verbs are "said" or "wrote". No inferences, not even obvious ones, are allowed on verbatim worlds. By default, rdf star triples and reification statements are considered verbatim.
